Why it works
6

Breathing near six times a minute lines your heart rate up with your breath. This strengthens the baroreflex, the sensor loop in your neck and chest that keeps pressure steady.

↓

A long, slow exhale stimulates the vagus nerve and shifts you from fight-or-flight toward rest-and-digest. Heart rate slows and blood vessels relax.

∞

Single sessions give a short dip. Daily practice over weeks is what studies link to lower resting readings, usually a few mmHg systolic, on top of diet and movement.

    For an accurate reading
    • Skip caffeine, smoking and exercise for 30 minutes first.
    • Sit quietly for 5 minutes, back supported, feet flat, legs uncrossed.
    • Rest your arm on a table with the cuff at heart level, on bare skin.
    • Take two readings a minute apart and log the average.
    • Measure at the same times each day, morning and evening.

    薬Medicines 101

    Know what you take, and why.

    Plain-language facts about the main families of blood pressure medicine, drawn from standard drug references. Many people need one alongside natural methods, and they work well together.

    For you

    General information only, not medical advice. The leaflet that comes with your medicine, your pharmacist and your doctor are the authority for you. Never stop, skip or change a dose without asking them first.

    No match. Check the spelling on your box, or ask your pharmacist which family your medicine belongs to.

    Interactions worth knowing
    Painkillers (NSAIDs)Ibuprofen, loxoprofen (Loxonin), naproxen and similar can raise blood pressure, weaken many BP medicines and strain the kidneys. Ask a pharmacist before regular use. Paracetamol (acetaminophen) is usually the gentler choice.
    Cold & flu remediesDecongestants such as pseudoephedrine and phenylephrine raise blood pressure. Look for products marked safe for high blood pressure.
    Licorice (kanzō 甘草)Found in many Kampo medicines, including kakkontō, and some sweets and teas. Large or regular amounts raise blood pressure and lower potassium.
    Ephedra (maō 麻黄)An ingredient in some Kampo cold remedies, kakkontō among them. It acts like a stimulant and can raise blood pressure and heart rate.
    GrapefruitRaises blood levels of some calcium channel blockers, especially nifedipine and felodipine. Check the leaflet for yours.
    PotassiumPotassium supplements and potassium-based salt substitutes can push potassium too high with ACE inhibitors, ARBs or spironolactone. Ask before using them.
    AlcoholAdds to the dizziness some BP medicines cause, and raises blood pressure over time.
    Taking blood pressure medicine well
    • Take it at the same time each day. Linking it to a habit, such as brushing your teeth, helps.
    • If you miss a dose, follow the leaflet or ask a pharmacist. Don't double up unless told to.
    • Stand up slowly, especially in the first weeks or after a dose change.
    • Keep logging readings. They show your doctor whether the dose is right.
    • Bring a list of everything you take to each appointment, including supplements, Kampo and eye drops. Your okusuri techō (お薬手帳) is ideal.
    • Feeling well doesn't mean you can stop. High blood pressure rarely has symptoms.
    Questions to ask your pharmacist
    • What is this medicine for, and how will I know it's working?
    • Is it better taken in the morning or at night?
    • Which side effects should I expect, and which mean I should call you?
    • Does it interact with anything else I take, including over-the-counter or Kampo products?
    • Do I need blood tests while taking it?
    Get urgent help if you have swelling of the lips, tongue, face or throat, trouble breathing, fainting, or chest pain. These need care straight away, whatever medicine you take.

    MedlinePlus drug information (US National Library of Medicine), and PMDA, Japan's medicines agency, which publishes patient guides for drugs approved in Japan.
